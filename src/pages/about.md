Public Theology is a publication of public theology, public witness and shared resources. It gathers writing on how faith meets public life: the commons, the neighbor, the stranger, and the questions a shared world puts to belief. Everything here is free to read.

## Vision

We hold that theology belongs in public, answerable to publics it does not control. David Tracy named three publics every theologian addresses: the academy, the church, and the wider society. Most theology speaks to the first two and forgets the third. Public Theology exists for the third, and it exists because the third is in trouble. The loudest public theologies in the United States today are theologies of domination; they are asserted rather than argued, and the lacuna between assertion and argument is where this publication does its work.

We envision a public theology that is inclusive, interreligious, and ecumenical. Inclusive, because the public is the whole public, including the counterpublics that the coffeehouse and the op-ed page have always excluded. Interreligious, because the questions that matter (land, water, war, the dignity of persons) are not the property of one tradition; the synagogue, mosque, longhouse, and temple sit at the table with the church. Ecumenical, because the Catholic, the Lutheran, the Baptist, and the Pentecostal are arguing about the same public whether or not they speak to each other.

We hold the field to be capacious and creative. The systematic theologian, the organizer, the legal scholar, the poet, the preacher, and the podcaster all do public theology; the address, not the form, makes it public. The pulpit of 2026 includes the feed and the stream, and we take them as seriously as any lectern.

We hold that argument about public life should itself be public. This site is the quick conversation, open to reply. [journal.publictheology.com](https://journal.publictheology.com) is the slower one: peer reviewed, open access, never paywalled. Neither is a pulpit. Both are tables, and the only criterion for a seat is the willingness to give reasons and to hear them.
