Digital Humanities (DH) is often framed as a technical field — data, metadata, platforms, tools. But for those of us doing public ministry, DH is not simply a method. It is a **site of theological struggle**, a place where power, narrative, and community collide. When we bring DH into the DMin classroom, we are not teaching pastors how to “use technology.” We are teaching them how to **see the digital as a contested theological landscape**.

Christian hegemony has shaped the digital world just as deeply as it has shaped our pulpits, seminaries, and denominational structures. The platforms we rely on — from search engines to social media to academic repositories — encode assumptions about authority, neutrality, and “the center.” DH gives us tools to expose those assumptions, to map them, and to imagine alternatives.

In my own work, I turn to DH not as a novelty but as a **decolonial practice**. Network graphs become ways of visualizing whose voices dominate theological discourse — and whose voices are systematically excluded. Metadata becomes a theological question: *Who gets named? Who gets tagged? Who gets archived?* Public-facing DH projects become pastoral interventions, creating space for communities to narrate themselves rather than be narrated by dominant traditions.

For DMin students, this work is transformative. They begin to see that public ministry is not only preaching and teaching — it is **curating, interpreting, and challenging the digital ecosystems that shape their congregations’ imaginations**. DH becomes a way to practice liberation theology in real time: naming power, disrupting hierarchy, and building alternative networks of meaning.

A DH-informed public theology refuses to accept the digital world as neutral. It insists that formation happens everywhere — in the feed, in the archive, in the algorithm. And it equips leaders to intervene with courage, creativity, and theological depth.

This is the work of resisting Christian hegemony. This is the work of public ministry. This is the work DH makes possible.
