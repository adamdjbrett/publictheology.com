Public Theology opens today. Martin Marty coined the term in 1974 to mark the difference between theology done in public and the "civil religion" that borrows religious symbols while belonging to no tradition; David Tracy later named the three publics every theologian addresses: the academy, the church, and the wider society. Most theology speaks to the first two and forgets the third. This publication exists for the third.

We publish three kinds of things. Public theology, meaning argued writing about how faith shows up in public life (in law, in markets, in schools, on screens, on the land). Public witness, meaning the testimony of people who do that work in their own communities and who rarely get the platform the loudest voices enjoy. Shared resources, meaning syllabi, bibliographies, and tools, added as they prove useful rather than on a schedule.

Two commitments shape what appears here. First, public theology is at its best when it is inclusive, interreligious, and ecumenical; the synagogue, mosque, and temple belong at the table alongside the church, and the Catholic, Lutheran, Baptist, and Pentecostal are arguing about the same public whether or not they talk to each other. Second, argument about public life should itself be public. The blog is the quick conversation; [journal.publictheology.com](https://journal.publictheology.com) is the slower, peer-reviewed, open-access one. Neither is a pulpit. Both are tables. All are welcome whether you are baptized or not, believe a little or a lot, or not at all.
