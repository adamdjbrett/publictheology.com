The moments standing in the middle of Palm Sunday's parade and the overturned tables in the temple are moments our church calendar never names. The crowd has shouted hosanna; the reckoning has not yet come. I've spent my ministry standing in that moment: at bedsides as a death doula, on a crisis line with [the Trevor project](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/), in coaching conversations about vocation, and through 21 years in the Navy. I have returned to [United Lutheran Seminary](https://www.uls.edu/) to give it a name.

 My conviction is simple. Change happens to people without their partnership. Transition is something people build together. Most churches right now are living through change. Geographer Michael Broadway [notes](https://ruralinsights.org/content/the-changing-church-landscape-of-the-upper-peninsula/) that weekly worship attendance nationally has fallen from 42 to 30% in two decades, and that median attendance in ELCA congregations dropped from 100 to 60 between 2000 and 2019. Tracing mergers and closers across Michigan’s Upper Peninsula back to 1966, he treats them as inevitable.

 I want to argue otherwise, and not about the numbers. Many churches have become rural in a deeper sense: they have pulled inward beneath what sociologist Peter Berger calls a sacred canopy, whether they sit on a country road or downtown. Geography is not what decides their future. Posture is: scarcity or abundance. And much of the help on offer, one fix for every church or fear sold as a product, takes away the very agency a congregation needs to discern what it wants to become.

 So this blog will look at churches as networks of trust. In digital humanities, a network is people or things (nodes) joined by relationships (edges). The sketch above draws 1 congregation twice. On the left, scarcity: a tight cluster that talks mostly to itself beneath the barrier while a ready made fix presses in. On the right, the same people keep every tie they had and begin building new ones with neighbors as trust allows. Some are formed; Some are still forming; Some neighbors have not yet Ben met and the invitation remains available.

 Diagrams and maps like this can mislead. Stanford's [Mapping the Republic of Letters](http://republicofletters.stanford.edu/) project charts thousands of enlightenment letters, but it cannot say what any tie meant, or who owed what to whom, the relational knowledge [Brown, Whaanga, and Lewis](https://dhdebates.gc.cuny.edu/read/debates-in-the-digital-humanities-2023/section/98dc1c8f-8583-4428-ac84-17ff072bdcad) insist on. A churches claim of “everyone is welcome” counts nodes. The [Mama Bears](https://www.realmamabears.org/), a community of mothers of LGBTQIA2S+ children, asks about edges instead: who may lead, whose weddings happen, whose names are used. ReconcilingWork’s [Reconciling in Christ](https://www.reconcilingworks.org/ric/) journey asks the same questions, and calls itself a journey begun from its “own unique context”, not a checklist.

 Expect posts on transition and trust; On queer inclusion, disability justice, and cultural justice; on what church records keep and miss; And on neighbors who, like [Cornelius for Peter](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2010&version=NRSVUE), turn out to be [where God already was](https://youtu.be/gUFZRkWfRIg).

 This blog lives in two places on purpose, [Humanities Commons](https://jmichaelmills.hcommons.org/) and [PublicTheology.com](http://PublicTheology.com). Sarah bond [warns](https://www.forbes.com/sites/drsarahbond/2017/01/23/dear-scholars-delete-your-account-at-academia-edu/) that [academia.edu](http://academia.edu), a for profit with an Edu name, profits from scholars who fill it. A nonprofit Commons answers to the people who use it, and I want this work to live where people are not the product.

 You do not need to tell me who you are to be part of this. Read quietly, or answer one of these in the comments:

Where has your church [been a protest march, and where a temple?](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2021&version=NRSVUE)

Who has shown you [God's image where you did not expect it](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2022%3A15-22%20&version=NRSVUE)?

I will listen before I build. We do not have to have arrived to be on the way together.
